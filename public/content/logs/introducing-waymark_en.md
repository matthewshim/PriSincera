# 🗺️ Starting Waymark — So You're Not Alone at the Fork

> A **decision journal + a companion that reaches out first + peer stories**, for people who make a lot of decisions. Here's why I'm building it, what it is, and the one line it will never cross.

I'm starting a new PriSincera product. Its name is **Waymark**. Not a single line of code runs yet — but I decided to **publish the thinking before building**. If you write the promise down first, you're less likely to drift from it while you build.

## 1. 💡 Why build this — deciding is lonelier than it looks

After nearly two decades as a planner, PO, and lead, I've learned one thing: we make countless decisions every day, yet **in the heavy moments we're alone**.

Should I change jobs, dissolve this team, pivot the product, stay in this relationship — the bigger the decision, the less anyone can carry it for you. The night you decide, you stare at the ceiling and ask, *"Is this the right call?"* And for days, for weeks, you quietly waver.

Two problems.

- **Advice is everywhere; company is not.** Search and answers pour out. But an answer isn't *company*. Often what you need isn't "do this" — it's **someone who feels the weight with you.**
- **Nothing is recorded, so nothing is learned.** A decision passes like an event. Months later, trying to recall "why did I do that," there's nothing left. So we repeat the **same regret** at the next fork.

> 💡 **In one line**: The trouble with decisions isn't "no right answer" — it's that **you're alone in the moment, and nothing remains afterward.**

---

## 2. 🧭 What Waymark is — four beats that never leave you alone

Waymark is not a tool that decides for you. It's a companion that **remembers** the decisions you've made, **reaches out first** when you waver, and **brings the story** of someone who faced the same fork. It moves in four beats.

1. **Capture** — Record the moment you decided, *and how you felt then.* Writing the emotion and context — not the outcome — matters: once we know how things turned out, we rewrite the past ("I knew it all along"). Keep the raw moment, and you can look back honestly later.
2. **Reach out** — Days later, weeks later, the service **comes to you first.** This is Waymark's heart. A notes app opens only when *you* open it, but people rarely turn to look at themselves when they're most shaken. So **company has to arrive first.**
3. **Stories** — A story comes from someone who **actually walked** the same fork. Not polished advice, but real, uneven experience. Empathy comes from the person who says, "I stood there too."
4. **Revisit** — In time you meet that decision again. Only when the loop of decision → outcome → reflection closes does **learning accumulate**, and the next decision gets a little less lonely.

> 💡 **In one line**: Not a single piece of advice, but a **relationship of capture–company–reflection** that builds up. That's where Waymark differs from a notes app or a chatbot.

---

## 3. 🛟 The one line it won't cross — it never decides for you

The most important principle: **Waymark never decides for you.**

Ask an AI "what should I do?" and it usually hands you an answer. Convenient. But in that instant, it's no longer **your** decision. Hand over the ownership of a decision and you lose three things with it — responsibility, self-trust, and the chance to learn from it.

So Waymark's AI **doesn't hand down the decision.** Instead it stands beside you in whatever tone the moment needs — **comfort** when your heart is heavy, **clarity** when thoughts tangle, **cheer** when you need to push forward, a careful **counsel** when you want the branches traced. But whatever the tone, the last sentence always returns to the same place:

> *"You know better than anyone which way fits you. I'll leave the choice to you."*

It **lights** the fork but never **walks it for you** — and no feature we add will ever change that line.

---

## 4. 🤝 The maker is the user

This is the service **I most need myself.** As someone who has made decisions for a living for almost twenty years, I too have passed through those lonely nights.

I build the problem I live — so **there's no authenticity to fake.** Not a focus group's imagination; user number one is me, and every feature is judged by whether I'd actually use it. I won't add a screen I wouldn't open, or a notification I'd find intrusive.

Of course this isn't only my story. **Anyone who has stood before a decision, big or small** — that's exactly the spot where Waymark wants to stand beside you.

---

## 5. 🏷️ About the name — a waymark, not a compass

**Waymark** = Way + Mark — a signpost standing at the fork.

A compass and a signpost differ. A compass **points** — "go that way." A signpost only **shows** where you are; it doesn't tell you where to go. What Waymark wants to be is the **signpost** — lighting up the spot you stand on, while you choose the path.

> Tagline: **"At every fork, beside you."**

---

## 6. 🌱 Not yet — and already

Honestly, the service is **not here yet.** The features and screens start from now.

I wrote this and raised an intro page first anyway, to **set down the promise before building.** "Never decide for you," "reach out first," "bring the story of someone who's been there" — these promises will be the measure of every choice ahead. And I'll record that process, raw, right here in the Builder's Log.

The day it's ready, **you** are the first I'll reach. At that fork — so you're not alone.
